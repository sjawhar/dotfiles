# Game story specifically

Game narrative has one constraint prose doesn't: a mechanic the player repeats hundreds of times
either carries the theme or fights it, and no amount of good dialogue fixes a mechanic working
against the feeling the story wants. That's why feeling and theme come before plot work in Phase 3
of the main skill, and why each pillar gets tested against the actual mechanic rather than treated
as pure writing.

## Feeling and theme first, mechanic as the carrier

*GRIS* is a wordless platformer about grief in which color returns to the world as the player
progresses. Nomada Studio deliberately avoided dialogue to leave "room to project their own
thoughts and interpretations"
([Vice, 2024-12-20](https://www.vice.com/en/article/nomada-studio-lets-me-in-to-learn-how-gris-and-neva-deliver-a-beautiful-and-touching-message-interview/)),
and its creative director said eliminating death and violence entirely was "in part" a pacing
decision, not only a tonal one
([Conrad Roset, 2019](https://thatgamersasylum.wordpress.com/2019/01/26/gris-creative-director-conrad-roset-interview/)).
Jenova Chen on *Journey*: the team built the game "like a Japanese garden," where every element is
there because it serves the feeling, not because it's a feature a platformer is expected to have.

## Story as the level-design anchor

*Celeste*'s "fractal story" structure gives the whole game, each area, and each individual screen
its own emotional arc, and pacing follows that curve rather than a difficulty curve alone (Maddy
Thorson, GDC 2017). A narrative beat sequence for a game should be checked against level or area
structure the same way — does each area's arc match what the story needs there, or was the level
built first and the story beat bolted on after.

## A one-page frame before any lore

Before building out a lore bible, settle: a logline, the core fantasy, two or three narrative
pillars each tied to a specific mechanic, one or two themes, and the tone. Blind Squirrel's 2025
narrative-design series makes the case for doing this before anything else: "having too much to
say results in not saying much at all"
([part 2](https://blindsquirrelentertainment.com/news/Narrative-Design-Part-2)). Only after that
frame exists does a lore bible — each character's want versus need, world history, faction
detail — get built on top of it.

## Human editors stay

GDC 2026's editing panel, on why a model can't do the final pass alone: "you cannot replace the
touch of a human. Editing is [...] emotional consistency"
([Game Developer, 2026-03-12](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/making-the-case-for-strong-human-narrative-editors)).
This is the same claim Phase 6 of the main skill makes for prose generally — the author's
line-by-line pass is not a step that gets automated away, in a game or anywhere else.
